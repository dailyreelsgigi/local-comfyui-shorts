Making a 46-second Cantonese short entirely in local ComfyUI

Documentation in English. The film itself is in Cantonese (burned-in subtitles).

What came out

  • 5 clips, 3–6 shots each, 1088×608, 24 fps, 46.7 s total
  • Dialogue and score generated locally: MiniMax H3 Ref2VA (picture + native Cantonese voice), ACE-Step 1.5 XL Turbo (BGM)
  • All post in ffmpeg: silence trimming, subtitle burn, BGM ducking, fade to black

    • Environment

  • ComfyUI, local; H3 Ref2VA nodes (T8 Hybrid: ReferenceToVideo + AddGuide)
  • Models: acestep_v1.5_xl_turbo_bf16, qwen_0.6b_ace15, qwen_4b_ace15, ace_1.5_vae
  • One 360-frame clip ≈ 13 minutes on this machine

    • The seven steps

  • Script — max 13 Chinese characters per line, one line per shot
  • Character refs — one turnaround sheet per person (one ID shot + full-body front/side/back); feed the original file, never a crop
  • Empty scene plate — generate one clean plate per location first (Nobody is anywhere in the frame, no people, no hands, no figures, no shadows of people.), then reuse that same plate in every shot
  • One six-section prompt per clip — subject_definitions / summary / retention_analysis / detailed_description / overall_soundscape / non_diegetic_music
  • Submit — 360 frames (15 s) per clip, one at a time
  • Trim silence + burn subtitles — silencedetect to find speech spans, keep 0.2 s either side
  • BGM + transitions — ACE-Step 60 s instrumental, ducked under dialogue; 0.4 s fade to black at scene changes

    Prompt skeleton (copy as-is)

    
[reference generation]
<Picture 1> is a turnaround sheet of the woman <Subject 1>: ...
<Picture 2> is a turnaround sheet of the man <Subject 2>: ...

subject_definitions:
<Subject 1> is the woman in her late twenties, ...
<Subject 2> is the man in his mid-thirties, ...

summary:
[Shot 1] ...
[Shot 2] ...

retention_analysis:
<Picture 1> <Picture 2> <Picture 3> are retained: keep the faces, hair and wardrobe exactly.

detailed_description:
[Shot 1] A wide two-shot on a covered footbridge at night, 35mm, locked off. ...
  <Subject 2> says in an unhurried voice, about four syllables a second, with a small pause at each comma, <d>[Cantonese] 我以前一個人行，都冇所謂。</d> and stops talking, lips closing. Nobody says anything more.
[Shot 2] ...

overall_soundscape:
Only two voices and the location sound: distant traffic, the hum of the ceiling lights.

non_diegetic_music:
No music at all in this video.

    ffmpeg chain (actually used)

    
# 1) find dead air; keep 0.2 s either side of each line
ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -af silencedetect=noise=-35dB:d=0.3 -f null -

# 2) BGM under dialogue, +3 dB when nobody speaks (sidechain ducking)
ffmpeg -y -i film.mp4 -i bgm.mp3 -filter_complex \
 '[0:a]aformat=sample_rates=32000:channel_layouts=stereo,asplit=2[sp][main];\
  [1:a]aformat=sample_rates=32000:channel_layouts=stereo,volume=-1dB,afade=t=in:st=0:d=2,afade=t=out:st=42.5:d=4[bg];\
  [bg][sp]sidechaincompress=threshold=0.02:ratio=4:attack=20:release=350[duck];\
  [main][duck]amix=inputs=2:duration=first:normalize=0[a]' \
 -map 0:v -map '[a]' -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 192k out.mp4

# 3) fade to black at scene changes: fade each segment's tail/head, then concat.
#    Do NOT chain several fade filters on one file — fade=t=in blacks out everything before st.

    Five things that bit me

  • Line lands on the wrong person — if the speaker is off frame, the model puts the line in the listener's mouth. Either keep the speaker in frame (profile or over-the-shoulder counts) or state only his voice is heard, her lips stay closed.
  • Duplicates — "his profile fills the left edge" makes the model draw a second man. For solo shots write Only one person is in this frame, no shoulder, no arm, no hand.
  • Drifting background — the scene description must be word-for-word identical in every shot, and you must feed the right empty plate (feeding the street plate instead of the bridge plate changes the location).
  • Speech rate — the model rushes on its own (measured 8 chars/sec in one take) and often ignores a "slow down" instruction. Verify by measuring chars ÷ seconds and re-render if it is over.
  • Subtitles — detect speech per shot window on the final timeline, never burn each clip separately before joining (trimming silence afterwards throws every cue off).
